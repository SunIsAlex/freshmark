6.1 High-pressure hydrogen storage. Compressed hydrogen is currently one of the most commonly used hydrogen storage methods. Hydrogen is stored in a container with a pressure maintained at 350~700 bar. Under high pressure, its behavior deviates from an ideal gas and is more suitable to be described by the van der Waals equation (p+a/Vm2)(Vm−b)=RT(p + a/V_{\text{m}}^2)(V_{\text{m}} - b) = RT, where VmV_{\text{m}} is the molar volume of the gas and pp is the gas pressure. Inorganic Chemistry Inference Questions: Fluorides and Oxides of Xenon

1. Transcription of the title

Non-metallic hexafluoride A is a colorless crystal that is stable at room temperature and has strong oxidizing properties. Gaseous A is a single molecule. Due to its slightly distorted structure, its molecular point group is C3vC_{3v}.

The process of A reacting with water is a multi-step hydrolysis. As the hydrolysis proceeds, three compounds B, C, and D are obtained in sequence. Binary compound D is a colorless needle-shaped crystal, which will be strongly decomposed under 25∘C25^\circ\mathrm{C} to obtain elemental substance E and combustion-supporting gas F.

D is soluble in KOH solution to generate anion G; passing O3\mathrm{O_3} into the dilute solution of G will generate another highly oxidizing regular octahedral anion H. At 298 K, H in acidic media rapidly oxidizes Mn(II)\mathrm{Mn(II)} to MnO4−\mathrm{MnO_4^-}.

N2F2\mathrm{N_2F_2} is heated together with E, and the main product is I. I is a colorless solid that sublimates easily at room temperature. At room temperature, I reacts strongly in aqueous solution, and a disproportionation reaction occurs, and D, E, F and HF can be obtained. But if I reacts with water under −80∘C-80^\circ\mathrm{C}, J will be obtained.

J is a bright yellow non-volatile solid, which slowly decomposes into equal amounts of C and K under −15∘C-15^\circ\mathrm{C}. A, I, K have the same elemental composition.

3-1

Write the chemical formula of the substance corresponding to the number.

3-2

If the alkaline aqueous solution of G is not passed through O3\mathrm{O_3}, part of H will also be produced, and the ratio of the amounts of E and H in the product is 1:11:1. Write an equation for this reaction.

3-3

J exists in solids as a chain structure formed by a planar quadrilateral trans structure through shared vertices. Draw a schematic diagram of a chain-like structure containing at least two repeating units.

3. Inference process

1. Judgment A

A is hexafluoride, the gaseous molecule is slightly distorted, the point group is C3vC_{3v}, and it has strong oxidizing property, therefore:

A=XeF6\boxed{A=\mathrm{XeF_6}}

2. Gradual hydrolysis of A

During the stepwise hydrolysis of xenon hexafluoride, two F atoms are replaced by one O atom at each step and two molecules of HF are produced.

Step one:

XeF6+H2O→XeOF4+2HF\mathrm{XeF_6+H_2O\rightarrow XeOF_4+2HF}

So:

B=XeOF4\boxed{B=\mathrm{XeOF_4}}

Step two:

XeOF4+H2O→XeO2F2+2HF\mathrm{XeOF_4+H_2O\rightarrow XeO_2F_2+2HF}

So:

C=XeO2F2\boxed{C=\mathrm{XeO_2F_2}}

Step three:

XeO2F2+H2O→XeO3+2HF\mathrm{XeO_2F_2+H_2O\rightarrow XeO_3+2HF}

So:

D=XeO3\boxed{D=\mathrm{XeO_3}}

The overall response is:

XeF6+3H2O→XeO3+6HF\mathrm{XeF_6+3H_2O\rightarrow XeO_3+6HF}

3. Determine E and F

Xenon trioxide decomposes to form elemental xenon and oxygen:

2XeO3→2Xe+3O2\mathrm{2XeO_3\rightarrow 2Xe+3O_2}

Therefore:

E=Xe\boxed{E=\mathrm{Xe}}

F=O2\boxed{F=\mathrm{O_2}}

Oxygen has combustion-supporting properties, which is consistent with the question conditions.

4. Determine G and H

Xenon trioxide dissolves in KOH solution to form hydrogen xenonate:

XeO3+OH−→HXeO4−\mathrm{XeO_3+OH^-\rightarrow HXeO_4^-}

Therefore:

G=HXeO4−\boxed{G=\mathrm{HXeO_4^-}}

G is further oxidized by ozone to generate perxenate radical with xenon +8+8 valence:

H=XeO64−\boxed{H=\mathrm{XeO_6^{4-}}}

There are six O around Xe in XeO64−\mathrm{XeO_6^{4-}}, and the spatial configuration is a regular octahedron.

The ozone oxidation reaction can be written as:

HXeO4−+O3+3OH−→XeO64−+O2+2H2O\mathrm{HXeO_4^-+O_3+3OH^- \rightarrow XeO_6^{4-}+O_2+2H_2O}

5. Judgment I

N2F2\mathrm{N_2F_2} is heated with xenon to mainly generate xenon tetrafluoride:

2N2F2+Xe→XeF4+2N2\mathrm{2N_2F_2+Xe\rightarrow XeF_4+2N_2}

Therefore:

I=XeF4\boxed{I=\mathrm{XeF_4}}

Xenon tetrafluoride is a colorless solid that sublimates easily, which is consistent with the description of the question.

At room temperature, xenon tetrafluoride undergoes a disproportionation reaction with water:

6XeF4+12H2O→2XeO3+4Xe+3O2+24HF\mathrm{6XeF_4+12H_2O \rightarrow 2XeO_3+4Xe+3O_2+24HF}

Products include:

XeO3, Xe, O2, HF\mathrm{XeO_3,\ Xe,\ O_2,\ HF}

That is D, E, F and HF in the question.

6. Judgment J

At −80∘C-80^\circ\mathrm{C}, xenon tetrafluoride undergoes partial hydrolysis:

XeF4+H2O→XeOF2+2HF\mathrm{XeF_4+H_2O\rightarrow XeOF_2+2HF}

Therefore:

J=XeOF2\boxed{J=\mathrm{XeOF_2}}

7. Judgment K

J slowly decomposes into equal amounts of C and K at −15∘C-15^\circ\mathrm{C}:

2XeOF2→XeO2F2+XeF2\mathrm{2XeOF_2\rightarrow XeO_2F_2+XeF_2}

Due to:

C=XeO2F2C=\mathrm{XeO_2F_2}

So:

K=XeF2\boxed{K=\mathrm{XeF_2}}

At the same time:

A=XeF6,I=XeF4,K=XeF2A=\mathrm{XeF_6},\qquad I=\mathrm{XeF_4},\qquad K=\mathrm{XeF_2}

All three contain only two elements, Xe and F, and meet the condition that "A, I, and K have the same elemental composition".
