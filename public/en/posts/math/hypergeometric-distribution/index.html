The Hypergeometric Distribution and Its Properties

Starting from the definition, the expectation and variance of the hypergeometric distribution are completely deduced with the help of two combination identities (reduced order formula and Vandermonde identity), and every step is well documented.

Definition

X∼H(n,K,N)X\sim{H(n,K,N)}, X is said to obey the hypergeometric distribution.

P(X=k)=CKkCN−Kn−kCNn(k=0,1,2,...,n)\boxed{P(X=k)=\frac{C_{K}^{k}C_{N-K}^{n-k}}{C_{N}^{n}}(k=0,1,2,...,n)}

Preliminary knowledge

Commonly used combined identities

  1. Cnk=nkCn−1k−1=n(n−1)k(k−1)Cn−2k−2=...C_{n}^{k}=\frac{n}{k}C_{n-1}^{k-1}=\frac{n(n-1)}{k(k-1)}C_{n-2}^{k-2}=...
  2. ∑k=0rCmkCnr−k=Cm+nr\sum_{k=0}^{r}C_{m}^{k}C_{n}^{r-k}=C_{m+n}^{r}

Proof 1:

Cnk=n!k!(n−k)!=nk(n−1)!(k−1)![(n−1)−(k−1)]!=nkCn−1k−1=n(n−1)k(k−1)Cn−2k−2\begin{gathered} C_{n}^{k}=\frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}\\=\frac{n}{k}\frac{(n-1)!}{(k-1)![(n-1)-(k-1)]!}\\=\frac{n}{k}C_{n-1}^{k-1}=\frac{n(n-1)}{k(k-1)}C_{n-2}^{k-2} \end{gathered}

Proof 2:

Consider this scenario: select r people (r≤min⁡m,n)(r\le \min{m,n}) among m boys and n girls. It is not difficult to find that 2 is the result of counting twice.

To add, 2 is exactly the famous Vandermonde Identity

Properties of random variables

D(X)=E(X2)−[E(X)]2\boxed{D(X)=E(X^2)-[E(X)]^2}

Starting from the definition of variance: D(X)=∑i=0npi[Xi−E(X)]2=∑i=0n(piXi2)−2E(X)∑i=0n(piXi)+[E(X)]2=E(X2)−[E(X)]2\begin{gathered} D(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}p_i[X_i-E(X)]^2\\ =\sum_{i=0}^n(p_iX_i^2)-2E(X)\sum_{i=0}^n(p_iX_i)+[E(X)]^2\\ =E(X^2)-[E(X)]^2 \end{gathered}

Properties

  1. E(X)=nKNE(X)=n\frac{K}{N}
  2. D(X)=nKNN−KNN−nN−1D(X)=n\frac{K}{N}\frac{N-K}{N}\frac{N-n}{N-1}

E(X)=∑i=0n[P(X=i)i]=∑i=0n[CKiCN−Kn−iCNni]apply combinatorial identity 1 to eliminate i=∑i=1n[KiCK−1i−1CN−Kn−iCNni]=K∑i=1n[CK−1i−1CN−Kn−iCNn]apply combinatorial identity 2=KCNnCN−1n−1apply combinatorial identity 1=nKN\begin{gathered} E(X)=\sum_{i=0}^n[P(X=i)i]\\ =\sum_{i=0}^n[\frac{C_{K}^{i}C_{N-K}^{n-i}}{C_{N}^{n}}i]\\ \text{apply combinatorial identity 1 to eliminate }i\\ =\sum_{i=1}^n[\frac{K}{i}\frac{C_{K-1}^{i-1}C_{N-K}^{n-i}}{C_{N}^{n}}i]\\ =K\sum_{i=1}^n[\frac{C_{K-1}^{i-1}C_{N-K}^{n-i}}{C_{N}^{n}}]\\ \text{apply combinatorial identity 2}\\ =\frac{K}{C_{N}^n}C_{N-1}^{n-1}\\ \text{apply combinatorial identity 1}\\ =\frac{nK}{N} \end{gathered}

E(X2)=∑i=0n[P(X=i)i2]=∑i=0n[CKiCN−Kn−iCNni2]apply combinatorial identity 1 to eliminate i=K∑i=1n[CK−1i−1CN−Kn−iCNni]=K∑i=1n[(i−1+1)CK−1i−1CN−Kn−iCNn]=K∑i=1n[(i−1)CK−1i−1CN−Kn−iCNn+CK−1i−1CN−Kn−iCNn]apply combinatorial identity 2=K∑i=1n[(i−1)CK−1i−1CN−Kn−iCNn]+KCN−1n−1CNn=K∑i=1n[(i−1)CK−1i−1CN−Kn−iCNn]+KnNapply combinatorial identity 1 to eliminate i−1=K∑i=2n[(K−1)CK−2i−2CN−Kn−iCNn]+KnNapply combinatorial identity 2=K[(K−1)CN−2n−2CNn]+KnN=K(K−1)n(n−1)N(N−1)+KnN=KnN[(K−1)(n−1)N−1+1]D(X)=E(X2)−[E(X)]2=KnN[(K−1)(n−1)N−1+1]−(KnN)2=KnN[N2−(K+n)N+KnN(N−1)]=nKNN−KNN−nN−1\begin{gathered} E(X^2)=\sum_{i=0}^n[P(X=i)i^2]\\ =\sum_{i=0}^n[\frac{C_{K}^{i}C_{N-K}^{n-i}}{C_{N}^{n}}i^2]\\ \text{apply combinatorial identity 1 to eliminate }i\\ =K\sum_{i=1}^n[\frac{C_{K-1}^{i-1}C_{N-K}^{n-i}}{C_{N}^{n}}i]\\ =K\sum_{i=1}^n[(i-1+1)\frac{C_{K-1}^{i-1}C_{N-K}^{n-i}}{C_{N}^{n}}]\\ =K\sum_{i=1}^n[(i-1)\frac{C_{K-1}^{i-1}C_{N-K}^{n-i}}{C_{N}^{n}}+\frac{C_{K-1}^{i-1}C_{N-K}^{n-i}}{C_{N}^{n}}]\\ \text{apply combinatorial identity 2}\\ =K\sum_{i=1}^n[(i-1)\frac{C_{K-1}^{i-1}C_{N-K}^{n-i}}{C_{N}^{n}}]+K\frac{C_{N-1}^{n-1}}{C_{N}^{n}}\\ =K\sum_{i=1}^n[(i-1)\frac{C_{K-1}^{i-1}C_{N-K}^{n-i}}{C_{N}^{n}}]+\frac{Kn}{N}\\ \text{apply combinatorial identity 1 to eliminate }i-1\\ =K\sum_{i=2}^n[(K-1)\frac{C_{K-2}^{i-2}C_{N-K}^{n-i}}{C_{N}^{n}}]+\frac{Kn}{N}\\ \text{apply combinatorial identity 2}\\ =K[(K-1)\frac{C_{N-2}^{n-2}}{C_{N}^{n}}]+\frac{Kn}{N}\\ =K(K-1)\frac{n(n-1)}{N(N-1)}+\frac{Kn}{N}\\ =\frac{Kn}{N}[\frac{(K-1)(n-1)}{N-1}+1]\\ D(X)=E(X^2)-[E(X)]^2\\ =\frac{Kn}{N}[\frac{(K-1)(n-1)}{N-1}+1]-(\frac{Kn}{N})^2\\ =\frac{Kn}{N}[\frac{N^2-(K+n)N+Kn}{N(N-1)}]\\ =n\frac{K}{N}\frac{N-K}{N}\frac{N-n}{N-1} \end{gathered}

Summary

At this point, we have completely derived the distribution law based on only two combination identities.

E(X)=nKN,D(X)=nKN⋅N−KN⋅N−nN−1.E(X)=n\frac{K}{N},\qquad D(X)=n\frac{K}{N}\cdot\frac{N-K}{N}\cdot\frac{N-n}{N-1}.

There are two key points in the whole process: one is to repeatedly use identity 1 to reduce the combination number and eliminate ii in the summation term with ii; the other is to use the Vandermonde identity to merge the summation into a single combination number. The reason why i2i^2 is split into (i−1)+1(i-1)+1 when calculating the variance is precisely to create i(i−1)i(i-1), a structure that can be reduced to two orders again. In essence, it is to find the factorial moment E[X(X−1)]E[X(X-1)].

Note p=K/Np=K/N, and the result can be written as E(X)=npE(X)=np, D(X)=np(1−p)⋅N−nN−1D(X)=np(1-p)\cdot\dfrac{N-n}{N-1}. Compared with np(1−p)np(1-p) of the binomial distribution, the extra factor N−nN−1\dfrac{N-n}{N-1} is called the finite population correction factor - it is less than 1, which depicts the fact that sampling without replacement makes the variance smaller; when N≫nN\gg n, the factor tends to 1, and the hypergeometric distribution is approximated as a binomial distribution.

Another derivation: indicator variables (added by Claude)

This section was added by Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, after the author's derivation. It is not written by the original author.

No combinatorial sums are needed. Number the KK special items among the NN as 1,…,K1,\dots,K and let Ij=1I_j=1 when special item jj is drawn. Then

X=I1+I2+⋯+IK.X=I_1+I_2+\cdots+I_K .

Each item is drawn with probability nN\frac nN, and two given items are both drawn with probability n(n−1)N(N−1)\frac{n(n-1)}{N(N-1)}. Hence

E(X)=K⋅nN,E(X)=K\cdot\frac nN,

D(X)=∑jD(Ij)+∑j≠lCov⁡(Ij,Il)=KnN(1−nN)+K(K−1)[n(n−1)N(N−1)−n2N2]=KnN⋅N−nN[1−K−1N−1]=nKN⋅N−KN⋅N−nN−1.\begin{aligned} D(X)&=\sum_j D(I_j)+\sum_{j\ne l}\operatorname{Cov}(I_j,I_l)\\ &=K\frac nN\Bigl(1-\frac nN\Bigr)+K(K-1)\Bigl[\frac{n(n-1)}{N(N-1)}-\frac{n^2}{N^2}\Bigr]\\ &=K\frac nN\cdot\frac{N-n}{N}\Bigl[1-\frac{K-1}{N-1}\Bigr] =n\frac KN\cdot\frac{N-K}{N}\cdot\frac{N-n}{N-1}. \end{aligned}

The negative covariance n(n−1)N(N−1)−n2N2<0\frac{n(n-1)}{N(N-1)}-\frac{n^2}{N^2}<0 is exactly why sampling without replacement lowers the variance, and it explains the finite population correction N−nN−1\frac{N-n}{N-1} directly.
