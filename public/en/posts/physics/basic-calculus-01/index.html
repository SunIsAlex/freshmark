Sequence limit

Introduction

Let an=1−10−n(n=1,2,3,...)a_n=1-10^{-n}(n=1,2,3,...)

It can be known that ana_n is getting closer and closer to 1, but when nn is infinite, is ana_n actually 1?

From this, we introduce the ϵ−N\epsilon-\N language from the perspective of error:

ϵ−N language

If there exists an p∈Rp\in \R such that: for any real number ϵ>0\epsilon\gt0, there is an integer N\N, such that for any n>Nn\gt N, ∣an−p∣<ϵ|a_n-p|\lt\epsilon, then pp is called the limit of the sequence ana_n, denoted as lim⁡n→∞an=p\lim_{n\to \infty}a_n=p. Otherwise, it is called the sequence ana_n without limit.

Function limit

Introduction

With the limit of the series (sequence), the limit of the function is naturally derived.

When x→0x\to 0, what is the value of f(x)=sin⁡xxf(x)=\frac{\sin x}{x}?

In the same way, the ϵ−δ\epsilon-\delta language is introduced

ϵ−δ language

If there is a p∈Rp\in \R such that: for any ϵ>0\epsilon\gt0, if there is δ>0\delta\gt0, such that when 0<x0−x<δ0\lt x_0-x\lt \delta, ∣f(x)−p∣<ϵ|f(x)-p|\lt\epsilon, then the left limit of f(x)f(x) at x0x_0 is called pp, recorded as lim⁡x→x0−f(x)=p\lim_{x \to x_0^-} f(x)=p

In the same way, it is easy to get the definition of right limit.

In addition, if lim⁡x→x0−f(x)=lim⁡x→x0+f(x)\lim_{x \to x_0^-} f(x)=\lim_{x \to x_0^+} f(x), then the limit of f(x)f(x) at x0x_0 is pp, denoted as lim⁡x→x0f(x)=p\lim_{x \to x_0} f(x)=p

The above ϵ−N language/ϵ−δ language is not the focus of this section

Useful limit judgment rule

Pinching theorem

If f(x)≤g(x)≤h(x)f(x)\le g(x)\le h(x), and f(x),h(x)f(x),h(x) limits exist and are the same, then f(x),h(x),g(x)f(x),h(x),g(x) limits exist and are the same.

Derivative

Introduction

Considering the function f(x)=x2f(x)=x^2, what is the instantaneous rate of change of the function at x=1x=1?

Let’s first consider the average rate of change from x=1x=1 to x=1+Δxx=1+\Delta x:

f(1+Δx)−f(1)Δx=(1+Δx)2−1Δx=2+Δx\frac{f(1+\Delta x)-f(1)}{\Delta x}=\frac{(1+\Delta x)^2-1}{\Delta x}=2+\Delta x

When Δx→0\Delta x\to 0, the average rate of change approaches 22, which is the instantaneous rate of change of f(x)=x2f(x)=x^2 at x=1x=1, that is, the derivative.

Derivative ↔Micro-Business

Differential is a small amount of change. The quotient of differential is called differential quotient.

Definition f′(x)=lim⁡Δx→0ΔfΔx=dfdxf'(x)=\lim_{\Delta x\to0}\frac{\Delta f}{\Delta x}=\frac{df}{dx}

Definition of derivative

If the limit

lim⁡Δx→0f(x0+Δx)−f(x0)Δx\lim_{\Delta x\to 0}\frac{f(x_0+\Delta x)-f(x_0)}{\Delta x}

exists, then f(x)f(x) is said to be differentiable at x0x_0, and this limit value is called the derivative of f(x)f(x) at x0x_0, denoted as f′(x0)f'(x_0) or dfdx∣x=x0\left.\dfrac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}x}\right|_{x=x_0}.

If f(x)f(x) is differentiable at every point in the domain of definition, then f′(x)f'(x) is called the derivative function of f(x)f(x), referred to as derivative.

The above strict definition is not the focus of this section, the important thing is to calculate the derivative.

Four arithmetic rules for derivatives

Rules

Assume that f(x),g(x)f(x),g(x) can all be derived, then:

  1. Addition and Subtraction Rule: (f(x)±g(x))′=f′(x)±g′(x)(f(x)\pm g(x))'=f'(x)\pm g'(x)
  2. Multiplication Rule: (f(x)g(x))′=f′(x)g(x)+f(x)g′(x)(f(x)g(x))'=f'(x)g(x)+f(x)g'(x)
  3. Division rule: (f(x)g(x))′=f′(x)g(x)−f(x)g′(x)[g(x)]2\left(\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}\right)'=\dfrac{f'(x)g(x)-f(x)g'(x)}{[g(x)]^2}, where g(x)≠0g(x)\ne 0

Memory Tips: Multiplication rule - "Lead before and after, add lead without lead, then lead after lead"; Division rule - "Lead up and down minus lead up and down, divide the square of the following".

Derivation of the multiplication rule

[f(x)g(x)]′=lim⁡Δx→0f(x+Δx)g(x+Δx)−f(x)g(x)Δx[f(x)g(x)]'=\lim_{\Delta x\to 0}\frac{f(x+\Delta x)g(x+\Delta x)-f(x)g(x)}{\Delta x}

Adding and subtracting the same term f(x)g(x+Δx)f(x)g(x+\Delta x):

=lim⁡Δx→0f(x+Δx)−f(x)Δx⋅g(x+Δx)+f(x)⋅g(x+Δx)−g(x)Δx=\lim_{\Delta x\to 0}\frac{f(x+\Delta x)-f(x)}{\Delta x}\cdot g(x+\Delta x)+f(x)\cdot\frac{g(x+\Delta x)-g(x)}{\Delta x}

=f′(x)g(x)+f(x)g′(x)=f'(x)g(x)+f(x)g'(x)

AI Summary (Sonnet 4.6)

Introduction to the Basics of Calculus (for physics competitions, focusing on practical applications) is divided into three major sections:

Limit: From the sequence limit (ε-N) to the function limit (ε-δ), master the pinch theorem and the four arithmetic rules, and be able to calculate common limits.

Derivatives: Understand the essence of "instantaneous rate of change is the limit", memorize the derivative tables of common functions, and master the three major operation rules of addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division.

Physics Application: Velocity v=r˙v=\dot{r}, acceleration a=r¨a=\ddot{r}, use parametric equations + derivation to deal with geometric problems such as circular motion.
