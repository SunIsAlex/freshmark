General expressions

  1. with the advent of — with the arrival or beginning of an important new development or period.

    • Example: With the advent of artificial intelligence, many routine tasks can now be automated.

  2. sportswear / an athletic outfit — clothing designed for exercise or sports; a set of clothes worn for an athletic activity.

    • Example: She changed into a comfortable athletic outfit before going for a run.

  3. savor the moment / relish the joy — fully enjoy and appreciate a pleasant experience or feeling.

    • Example: After receiving the good news, she paused to savor the moment.

  4. a lecture delivered by — a formal educational talk given by a particular speaker.

    • Example: We attended a lecture delivered by a leading climate scientist.

  5. be tailored to/for — be specially designed or adapted to suit a particular person, group, or purpose.

    • Example: The course is tailored to the needs of advanced learners.

  6. respond / give a response — reply to a question, statement, or situation.

    • Example: The company gave a prompt response to the customer's complaint.

  7. be fascinated by / be intrigued by — be extremely interested in something; be curious about something unusual or mysterious.

    • Example: The students were fascinated by the scientist's account of life in Antarctica.

  8. be captivated by / be mesmerized by / be obsessed with — be strongly attracted and interested; be unable to look or think away; think about something excessively. Be obsessed with often has a negative connotation.

    • Example: The audience was captivated by her powerful performance.

  9. catch a glimpse of / get an overview of — see something briefly or incompletely; gain a general understanding of a subject.

    • Example: Readers can get an overview of modern economics from the introductory chapter.

  10. excel at/in / be proficient in / be adept at — perform exceptionally well at an activity or in a field; have a high level of skill; be naturally or highly skilled at doing something.

    • Example: She excels at solving complex problems and is proficient in three programming languages.

  11. gain deep insights into — develop a thorough and perceptive understanding of a subject.

    • Example: Fieldwork allowed the researchers to gain deep insights into local customs.

  12. give / deliver a lecture — present a formal educational talk. Deliver a lecture is slightly more formal than give a lecture.

    • Example: Professor Wang will deliver a lecture on classical Chinese poetry.

  13. long to do / yearn to do / itch to do — strongly want to do something. Itch to do is more informal and suggests impatience or eagerness.

    • Example: After months of preparation, the young athlete was itching to compete.

  14. seek to do / make an effort to do / do one's utmost to do — try to achieve something; make a deliberate attempt; try as hard as possible.

    • Example: We should do our utmost to protect endangered species.

  15. devote / commit / dedicate oneself to doing something — give a great deal of time, energy, or loyalty to an activity or cause.

    • Example: She dedicated herself to improving education in rural communities.

  16. seize an opportunity — act quickly to take advantage of a favorable situation. Chance can mean possibility, luck, or an occasion, whereas opportunity specifically means a favorable occasion.

    • Example: He seized the opportunity to study abroad for a year.

  17. a viable alternative — a practical option that can be chosen instead of another. Alternative is not always interchangeable with choice: a choice is the act or range of choosing, while an alternative is one of the other options.

    • Example: Solar power offers a viable alternative to fossil fuels.

  18. the very essence of / the quintessence of — the most fundamental quality of something; the purest or most perfect example of a quality or type.

    • Example: Mutual trust is the very essence of a healthy relationship.

  19. personal charm / glamour / charisma — an appealing personal quality; an attractive and exciting quality often linked to beauty or fame; a compelling presence that inspires admiration or loyalty.

    • Example: The speaker's warmth and charisma immediately won over the audience.

  20. an increasing number of people — more and more people; a steadily growing number of individuals.

    • Example: An increasing number of people are choosing to work remotely.

  21. older adults / older people / senior citizens — respectful terms for people in later life. Senior citizens is common in public-policy contexts; the aged can sound impersonal or outdated.

    • Example: The community center offers free technology classes for older adults.

  22. young people / adolescents — people who are young; young people in the stage between childhood and adulthood. Youngsters is informal, while youths may sound official or negative in some contexts.

    • Example: The program helps adolescents develop healthy study habits.

  23. challenging — requiring considerable effort, skill, or determination. It is more positive than difficult when a task is demanding but potentially rewarding.

    • Example: Learning to write well in another language is challenging but rewarding.

  24. a rich source of / an abundance of — something that supplies a large amount of valuable material; a very large quantity of something. Abundant cannot replace rich in every context.

    • Example: The region has an abundance of natural resources and a rich cultural heritage.

  25. prominent / eminent / distinguished — important and well known; highly respected within a profession; respected and admired for notable achievements.

    • Example: The award was presented to a distinguished historian.

  26. useful / beneficial / practical — helpful for a purpose; producing a positive effect; suitable and effective in real situations. Available means obtainable or ready for use, not useful.

    • Example: The workshop gave students practical advice on managing their time.

  27. in terms of — with regard to a particular aspect or from a particular point of view.

    • Example: The two proposals are similar in terms of cost and feasibility.

  28. in my view / as far as I am concerned / from my perspective — expressions used to introduce a personal opinion. In my view is concise and appropriate in most formal writing.

    • Example: In my view, public transport should be both affordable and accessible.

  29. have / exert a positive or negative influence on — affect someone or something in a helpful or harmful way.

    • Example: Supportive teachers can exert a positive influence on a student's confidence.

  30. the advantages far outweigh the disadvantages — the benefits are much greater or more important than the drawbacks.

    • Example: Although remote work has limitations, its advantages often far outweigh the disadvantages.

  31. do harm to / be harmful to / be detrimental to — damage or negatively affect someone or something. Be detrimental to is the most formal of the three.

    • Example: Excessive screen time can be detrimental to sleep quality.

  32. be beneficial to / be conducive to — have a positive effect on; help create conditions that make a particular result likely.

    • Example: A quiet environment is conducive to focused study.

  33. lead to / give rise to / contribute to / result in — cause a result; cause something, often something significant or negative, to emerge; be one of several causes; produce a particular outcome.

    • Example: A lack of exercise can contribute to a range of health problems.

Chinese culture, arts, and education

  1. attach great importance to — consider something extremely important and give it serious attention.

    • Example: Many schools attach great importance to students' all-round development.

  2. be hailed as — be publicly praised or recognized as a particular kind of person or thing.

    • Example: The novel has been hailed as a masterpiece of contemporary literature.

  3. a gem of Chinese culture — an especially valuable, beautiful, or admired part of Chinese culture.

    • Example: Kunqu Opera is widely regarded as a gem of Chinese culture.

  4. be exposed to Chinese culture — have opportunities to experience or learn about Chinese culture.

    • Example: Exchange students can be exposed to Chinese culture through calligraphy and local festivals.

  5. an embodiment of Chinese wisdom — something that expresses or represents the accumulated wisdom of Chinese civilization.

    • Example: The traditional calendar is an embodiment of Chinese wisdom about nature and seasonal change.

  6. promote / share Chinese culture — encourage wider awareness and appreciation of Chinese culture; introduce it to others.

    • Example: The exhibition aims to share Chinese culture with an international audience.

  7. preserve and carry on a cherished tradition — protect a valued tradition, continue practicing it, and pass it on to future generations.

    • Example: The annual celebration helps the community preserve and carry on a cherished tradition.

  8. traditional Chinese virtues — moral qualities long valued in Chinese society, such as integrity, respect, diligence, and filial devotion.

    • Example: The story highlights traditional Chinese virtues such as honesty and perseverance.

  9. traditional Chinese cultural elements — recognizable features, symbols, practices, or artistic forms associated with traditional Chinese culture.

    • Example: The designer incorporated traditional Chinese cultural elements into the modern building.

  10. cultural heritage — traditions, knowledge, objects, and historic places passed down through generations.

    • Example: Protecting cultural heritage helps communities maintain a sense of identity.

  11. help shape responsible, well-rounded citizens — help people acquire the knowledge, abilities, values, and social awareness needed to contribute to society.

    • Example: Education should prepare students for work while helping shape responsible, well-rounded citizens.

  12. a broad range of competencies / well-rounded development — a varied set of knowledge, skills, and abilities; balanced growth across intellectual, social, physical, and moral areas.

    • Example: Project-based learning helps students develop a broad range of competencies.

  13. exquisite / intricate craftsmanship — exceptionally beautiful and skillful workmanship; workmanship involving many fine and complex details.

    • Example: The wooden screen is admired for its intricate craftsmanship.

  14. cultural landmarks — important and recognizable places associated with a community's culture or history.

    • Example: The ancient temple is one of the city's best-known cultural landmarks.

  15. time-honored customs — traditions or practices that have been respected and followed for a very long time.

    • Example: Families observe time-honored customs during the Spring Festival.

  16. a historic site — a place important because of events, people, or developments in history. Historic emphasizes importance; historical simply means related to the past.

    • Example: The government has introduced new measures to preserve the historic site.

  17. aesthetic education — education that develops the ability to understand, appreciate, and create beauty and art.

    • Example: Music and painting play an important role in aesthetic education.

  18. enrich one's spirit / cultivate one's mind — deepen one's inner emotional or spiritual life; develop one's intellect, judgment, and character.

    • Example: Reading great literature can cultivate the mind and enrich the spirit.

  19. give / stage / put on / present a performance — perform a work for an audience. Stage, put on, and present can also refer to organizing the entire production.

    • Example: The drama club will put on a performance at the end of the semester.

  20. an artistic treasure / a treasure of the art world — a work of art that is considered exceptionally precious or important.

    • Example: This ancient scroll is an artistic treasure of immense historical value.

  21. tug at one's heartstrings / touch one's heart — evoke strong feelings of sympathy, tenderness, or sadness.

    • Example: The film's portrayal of family separation tugged at the audience's heartstrings.

  22. exquisite craftsmanship — extremely beautiful and skillful workmanship.

    • Example: Each piece of porcelain reflects the exquisite craftsmanship of local artisans.

  23. aesthetically pleasing — attractive or satisfying to look at, hear, or experience.

    • Example: The architects created a space that is both functional and aesthetically pleasing.

  24. broaden one's horizons — expand one's knowledge, experience, or understanding of the world.

    • Example: Traveling abroad can broaden your horizons and challenge your assumptions.

  25. enrich people's cultural lives — give people more varied and meaningful opportunities to experience culture and the arts.

    • Example: Free public concerts can enrich people's cultural lives.

  26. cultivate / refine one's aesthetic sensibility — develop or improve one's ability to recognize and appreciate beauty and artistic quality.

    • Example: Exposure to different art forms can refine students' aesthetic sensibility.

  27. promote students' moral, intellectual, physical, and aesthetic development — support balanced growth in character, knowledge, health, and appreciation of beauty.

    • Example: A well-designed curriculum should promote students' moral, intellectual, physical, and aesthetic development.

Artificial intelligence and technology

  1. algorithm — a precise set of rules or steps used to solve a problem or perform a calculation.

    • Example: The recommendation algorithm suggests films based on a user's viewing history.

  2. artificial intelligence (AI) — the ability of computer systems to perform tasks that normally require human intelligence.

    • Example: Artificial intelligence is increasingly used to analyze medical images.

  3. deep learning — a type of machine learning that uses multilayered neural networks to learn complex patterns from data.

    • Example: Deep learning has greatly improved the accuracy of speech recognition.

  4. neural network — a computing model made of connected processing units that is loosely inspired by the structure of the human brain.

    • Example: The team trained a neural network to identify objects in photographs.

  5. dataset — an organized collection of related data used for analysis or model training.

    • Example: The researchers removed duplicate records from the dataset before training the model.

  6. big data — extremely large and complex collections of data that require advanced tools to store, process, and analyze.

    • Example: Retailers use big data to better understand customer behavior.

  7. chatbot — a computer program designed to conduct a conversation with a person, usually through text or speech.

    • Example: The bank's chatbot can answer basic questions at any time of day.

  8. optimization — the process of making a system, design, or method as effective or efficient as possible.

    • Example: Route optimization can reduce both delivery times and fuel consumption.

  9. computation — the use of mathematical operations or computer processing to calculate or determine something.

    • Example: Training a large model requires enormous amounts of computation.

  10. robotics — the field concerned with designing, building, programming, and operating robots.

    • Example: Advances in robotics are making warehouse automation safer and more efficient.

  11. write programs for AI systems — create computer code that enables AI systems to perform specific tasks.

    • Example: Students learned to write programs for AI systems that recognize simple visual patterns.

  12. chat with AI assistants — have a text- or voice-based conversation with AI systems designed to help users. AI assistant is more natural for a user-facing conversational tool; AI agent often implies greater autonomy.

    • Example: Travelers can chat with AI assistants to plan an itinerary.

  13. operate humanoid robots — control or use robots whose form or behavior resembles that of humans.

    • Example: Technicians are trained to operate humanoid robots safely.

  14. train AI models — expose AI models to data so that they learn patterns and improve their performance on a task.

    • Example: Engineers used multilingual data to train the AI models.

  15. tune an AI model / adjust model settings — modify a model or its configurable settings to improve or change its performance and behavior.

    • Example: The researchers tuned the AI model to improve its prediction accuracy.

  16. test AI systems — evaluate AI systems to determine whether they are accurate, reliable, safe, and fit for purpose.

    • Example: Independent experts should test AI systems before they are widely deployed.

  17. use AI as an assistive tool / use AI for assistance — use AI to support human work rather than necessarily replace it.

    • Example: Doctors can use AI as an assistive tool when reviewing medical scans.

  18. monitor AI behavior — continuously observe how an AI system acts and performs, especially after deployment.

    • Example: Developers monitor AI behavior to detect unexpected or harmful outputs.

  19. update AI software — install or develop newer versions of software used by an AI system.

    • Example: Organizations should regularly update AI software to address security weaknesses.

  20. ethical boundaries / ethical principles — limits on what is considered morally acceptable; standards that guide responsible decisions and conduct.

    • Example: Clear ethical principles should guide the development and use of facial recognition.

Health and fitness

  1. increase blood oxygen levels — raise the amount or saturation of oxygen carried in the blood.

    • Example: Supplemental oxygen may increase blood oxygen levels in patients with certain respiratory conditions.

  2. improve cardiovascular health — strengthen the health and function of the heart and blood vessels.

    • Example: Regular aerobic exercise can improve cardiovascular health.

  3. build muscular strength — increase the amount of force that muscles can produce. Muscular strength is more idiomatic in fitness contexts than muscle strength after build.

    • Example: Resistance training helps build muscular strength and protect bone health.

  4. burn calories — use energy stored in the body through physical activity or normal bodily processes.

    • Example: Brisk walking is a simple way to burn calories and stay active.

  5. flexibility / agility — the ability to move joints and muscles through a wide range; the ability to move quickly and change direction with control.

    • Example: Yoga can improve flexibility, while shuttle drills develop agility.

  6. set fitness goals — establish specific physical-health or exercise objectives to work toward.

    • Example: Setting realistic fitness goals can help people stay motivated.

  7. train under professional guidance — exercise with instruction or supervision from a qualified professional.

    • Example: Beginners should train under professional guidance when learning complex weightlifting techniques.

Events and activities

  1. an environmental fair / an eco-themed carnival — a public event featuring enjoyable activities and information about environmental protection and sustainable living.

    • Example: The school held an eco-themed carnival to promote recycling and energy conservation.

  2. organize a series of workshops and demonstrations — arrange several practical learning sessions and displays that show how something is done.

    • Example: The museum organized a series of workshops and demonstrations on traditional crafts.

  3. provide a range of interactive stations — offer several activity areas where participants can learn through direct involvement.

    • Example: The science fair will provide a range of interactive stations for children.

  4. a storytelling session — an organized period during which stories are told or read to an audience.

    • Example: The library hosts a weekly storytelling session for young children.
